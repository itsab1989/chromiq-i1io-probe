ChromIQ X-Rite instrument probe
===============================

For the i1iO table, and since version 1.5 also for handheld
instruments: i1Pro, i1Pro 2, i1Pro 3, ColorMunki and i1Studio.

Thank you for helping!  This takes about two minutes.


THE SHORT VERSION
-----------------

1. Plug in your instrument (the i1iO table, or a handheld i1Pro,
   ColorMunki or i1Studio) and switch it on.

2. Double-click the file for your system:

       macOS    ->  Start-macOS.command
       Windows  ->  Start-Windows.bat
       Linux    ->  Start-Linux.sh

3. Follow what it says on screen.  After a minute it shows what is
   plugged in and asks what you would like to do: record the i1iO
   table, record a handheld instrument, or just make the report.
   Press Enter to take its suggestion.

4. It saves a text file called  chromiq-io-probe-<date>.txt
   Have a look at it, then (for the i1iO) attach it to:

       https://github.com/itsab1989/ChromIQ/issues/129

That's it.  You're done.


IS IT SAFE?
-----------

Yes.  It never sends anything to the table and it CANNOT move the arm.
It only reads information your computer already has about plugged-in
hardware.  Nothing will move.  Nothing is changed.


IF YOUR COMPUTER REFUSES TO OPEN IT
-----------------------------------

That is normal for downloaded files and does not mean anything is wrong.

   macOS  :  Double-click it once. When macOS refuses, open System
             Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down, click "Open
             Anyway", and confirm with "Open". (On macOS 14 and older:
             right-click the file, choose "Open", then "Open" again.)
   Windows:  Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
   Linux  :  Open a terminal here and run:  bash Start-Linux.sh


HANDHELD INSTRUMENT (i1Pro, i1Pro 2, i1Pro 3, ColorMunki, i1Studio)?
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Plug it in and run the tool as above. When it sees your instrument
and no i1iO table, it offers a guided recording session: about 15
minutes of short steps in the program you normally measure with
(calibrate, scan one strip, scan it again faster and slower, and so
on). Each step is recorded into its own small file; you can skip or
repeat any step and stop at any time without losing finished steps.

On Windows it needs the free USBPcap (comes with Wireshark; the tool
tells you exactly how to install it, and no security setting has to be
switched off), and it must run as administrator for the recording:
right-click it and choose "Run as administrator". The tool can fetch
USBPcap's official installer for you and start it, and can start itself
again as administrator, but only if you say yes each time. At the end the
window stays open until you press Enter, so you can read what to do next. If ChromIQ or ArgyllCMS installed its own driver for
the instrument, the measuring program cannot see it until X-Rite's
driver is back; the tool notices that and says how. The recording contains your
instrument's serial number and calibration data, because the program
reads them every time; the tool says so first, keeps only the
instrument's traffic in the folder you would send, and uploads
nothing. Nothing is promised: it helps us understand, that is all.

To send the session folder (as a zip, with the report): attach it in
the forum thread where you heard about this tool, or open a new issue
at  https://github.com/itsab1989/ChromIQ/issues/new  and attach it
there. The zip contains your instrument's serial number and its
factory calibration data; if you would rather not post that in
public, send it in a private message to itsab1989 on the forum.
Issue #129 is for the i1iO table only.
README.md has the details.


IMPORTANT FOR i1Pro 2 AND i1iO 2 OWNERS
---------------------------------------

i1Profiler 3.8.7 (July 2026) removed support for the i1Pro 2 and the
i1iO 2.  Stay on i1Profiler 3.8.6 or older and do not let it update.


MORE DETAIL
-----------

README.md answers everything else: what it collects, what it does
about your privacy, what to do if it goes wrong, and an optional
recording step you are very welcome to skip.  On a Mac the tool tells
you honestly whether a recording is possible there at all.
